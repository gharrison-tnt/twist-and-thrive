Go-to-market strategy and digital presence build for a nonprofit launching a children's mental health literacy program. Includes positioning, audience mapping, channel strategy, and a phased activation roadmap.

The Starting Point

Strong mission. Invisible reach.

Youth Advocacy Alliance was doing meaningful, differentiated work — but operating as though word-of-mouth alone would carry a public health message. There was no consistent positioning, no clear articulation of who the program was for, and no channel strategy for reaching the communities, schools, and families who needed it most.

The gap wasn't in the program. It was in how the program communicated its value — and to whom.

Core finding

"The program had strong outcomes and zero visibility. The work was sound. The infrastructure around it wasn't."

Audience Mapping

Two audiences. One message architecture.

The organization was trying to reach everyone — and landing with no one. The audit identified two distinct audiences with different motivations, different channels, and different definitions of value.

Primary — Institutional

School Districts & Community Partners

  • K-12 administrators, counselors, PTAs
  • Pain: reactive mental health support, no preventive framework
  • Decision driver: evidence of outcomes + ease of implementation
  • Channel: direct outreach, district partnerships, educator events

Secondary — Community

Families & Caregivers

  • Parents of children 6–16, community members
  • Pain: lack of language and tools to talk about mental health at home
  • Decision driver: trust, accessibility, peer recommendation
  • Channel: social media, community events, faith communities

Positioning

The one thing to own in the market.

The previous framing centered on what the organization was rather than what it made possible for the people it served. Positioning was reoriented around the audience's outcome, not the organization's identity.

Before

"A nonprofit advancing children's mental health awareness."

After

"Giving kids the language to name what they feel — and adults the tools to respond."

This positioning works across both audiences: it speaks to the educator who needs a program students can actually use, and to the parent who doesn't know how to start the conversation. One message. Two doors in.

Activation Roadmap

The first moves, in order.

1
Weeks 1–2: Positioning & Digital Foundation

Finalize positioning language. Build and launch website with clear audience pathways — one for educators/districts, one for families. Establish social channels with consistent voice and visual identity.

2
Weeks 3–4: Institutional Outreach

Build targeted list of school counselors and curriculum directors across 10 districts. Begin personalized outreach with a one-page program overview. Goal: 5 intro conversations in 30 days.

3
Month 2: Community Activation

Launch social content calendar. Identify 2–3 community organizations (faith communities, parent networks, recreation centers) as referral and event partners. Host the first free parent workshop.

4
Month 3: Proof & Momentum

Gather testimonials from pilot partners and workshop attendees. Package outcomes data into a one-page impact brief for grant applications and district proposals. Confirm pilot commitments and set year-one enrollment targets.
